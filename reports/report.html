2. Client mandate and Investment Policy Statement

Client and horizon

The fund is a perpetual USD 10M endowment supporting scholarships, research and faculty development. A perpetual fund can wait out a market fall, but it cannot stop paying scholarships during one. We therefore judge the return objective over rolling 10-year periods, monitor risk daily and over 10 days, and budget risk per crisis episode.

Return objective

The fund must pay for its spending, hold its real value and grow a little. We set spending at 3.0% of a 12-quarter average value (range considered 2.5–3.5%), add 0.5% real growth, and use the 10-year breakeven inflation rate at the decision date.

Required return = (1 + 3.0%) × (1 + 0.5%) × (1 + 2.34%) − 1 = 5.94% a year

Risk constraints

One preference sets the risk limits: the most the committee will lose in any crisis, historical or designed. We set this crisis budget at 25% (range considered 20–30%). The frontier point whose crisis loss equals 25% then fixes the volatility limit (8.29%) and the 99% ES limit (2.26%), so the limits cannot contradict one another. An earlier draft (volatility 8%, crisis loss 20%, ES 1.5%) was replaced because its three limits implied three different equity shares.

3. Data

Sources

All data come from the approved sources. Prices are Yahoo Finance adjusted closes for the seven ETFs, through yfinance. Rates, inflation expectations and stress factors are FRED series (10-year and 2-year Treasury, 10-year TIPS, breakeven inflation, 3-month T-bill, Baa spread, oil, dollar index, VIX, CPI). Sri Lankan inflation is World Bank data, used for context. The equity premium is a disclosed assumption, not data.

The raw download is frozen in data/raw/ with a SHA-256 hash for every file, so the judges can rebuild every number from the repository.

Cleaning

  • Period: 18 November 2004, when GLD began trading, to 26 October 2026: 5,518 trading days.
  • Calendars: all seven ETFs trade on US exchanges, so the inner join on trading dates dropped 0 days. FRED series have their own holidays and were carried forward by at most 3 business days.
  • Adjusted prices: used throughout, so dividends and splits do not distort returns. We verified this against close prices plus dividends.
  • Log and simple returns: log returns for statistics on one series; simple returns wherever assets are combined, because only simple returns add across assets.
  • Risk-free rate: the 3-month T-bill, lagged one day, so no estimate uses information from the same day.
  • Partition: development 2004–2018, validation 2019–2022, final test 2023–2026. Every selection used data to 2022 only.

Suspect points are flagged and kept, never deleted. Every one matches a real event, such as the AGG fall of 10 October 2008 and the COVID crash, and none is a data error. Deleting them would remove the crises the fund must survive.

4. Portfolio construction

Method

We start from Markowitz mean-variance and CAPM, but on these assets textbook optimisation pushes the portfolio into corners, because small errors in expected returns become large positions. We therefore separate the two jobs. The frontier minimises variance at each equity share under the caps, using a Ledoit-Wolf covariance matrix on 756 days. Expected returns are then used only to score the points.

min wᵀΣw subject to Σ wᵢ = 1, wᵢ ≥ 0, Σ_equity wᵢ = e, wᵢ ≤ capᵢ

Expected returns are built from yields on the decision date, not from past returns. AGG earns the 10-year yield plus 0.5%. Equities earn the yield plus beta times a 4.5% premium (CAPM). Gold earns breakeven inflation. Fees, withholding tax, trading costs and the variance drag ½wᵀΣw are deducted.

Selection rule

The locked rule picks the lowest-risk frontier point that meets the objective inside every limit. It gives ACCEPT at 35% equity: SPY 15.6%, EFA 9.4%, AGG 65.0%, XLE 10.0%.

The trade-off

Moving from 30% to 35% equity adds 0.23 points of return, about USD 23,800 a year, for 2.0 points more crisis loss. Moving from 35% to 40% adds only 0.08 points, about USD 7,700 a year, for 1.5 points more crisis loss, and breaks the budget. The gain shrinks because of what equity replaces. Up to 35% it replaces gold, which is assumed to earn only breakeven inflation. Above 35% the gold is gone and it replaces AGG, which yields about 5.5%. So 35% is both the first point that meets the objective and the point where more risk starts to pay much less.

Conventional alternatives

At the decision date, equal weight, 60/40, maximum Sharpe and risk parity all break the crisis budget (losses of 28–42%). Global minimum variance and minimum CVaR stay inside it but are expected to earn only 4.3–4.5%, short of the objective.

5. Risk measurement

Definitions

VaR and ES are positive numbers meaning losses. Losses are computed on point-in-time weights, with simple returns aggregated across assets.

VaRα = −inf { x : P(R ≤ x) ≥ 1 − α }

ESα = 1 / (1 − α) × ∫ from α to 1 of VaRᵤ du

Three methods, and why they disagree

We compute historical simulation, parametric (normal, Student-t, Cornish-Fisher) and Monte Carlo VaR at 95% and 99%, plus EVT and GARCH-based models, on the same recommended weights.

  • At 95% the historical, parametric and Monte Carlo methods nearly agree (USD 49,000–61,000). This part of the distribution is well observed.
  • At 99% they do not (USD 88,000–115,000, and USD 212,000 for Cornish-Fisher). Among these methods the normal model is the highest at 95% and the lowest at 99%: tail events inflate its variance, which raises the centre, while its thin tails miss the extremes.
  • Monte Carlo draws from a fitted Student-t and lands beside the parametric t model, because it shares the same assumption. Simulation adds no information the distribution does not already hold.
  • Volatility-aware models are lower because they respond to today's calm market, while historical models still remember every bad day in their two-year window.
  • Cornish-Fisher is unreliable here. Its correction is valid only for mild skew and kurtosis, and these returns are too fat-tailed. We show it for comparison and do not use it.

These figures describe a calm market. Through the cycle, on 2004–2018 data including the 2008 crisis, the average very bad day costs USD 208,000, which is the figure tested against the limit.

Why Expected Shortfall as well

VaR says where a bad day starts and nothing about how bad it gets. It is also not subadditive, so two positions can show more VaR together than apart (Artzner et al., 1999), as a two-bond example in notebook 03 shows. ES averages the whole tail and is coherent. The committee's tail limit is therefore set on ES, with VaR kept because regulators' backtests are built on it.

Extreme Value Theory

We fit a Generalised Pareto distribution to losses above a threshold u (peaks over threshold; Balkema and de Haan, 1974; Pickands, 1975), by maximum likelihood with n observations and Nᵤ exceedances.

VaRₚ = u + (β / ξ) × { [ (n / Nᵤ)(1 − p) ]^(−ξ) − 1 }

ESₚ = (VaRₚ + β − ξu) / (1 − ξ)

The threshold was not chosen arbitrarily. Six candidates were compared on parameter stability, Anderson-Darling and Kolmogorov-Smirnov fit, and exceedance counts, and three selection rules were compared. We fixed 90% on the candidate portfolio before the decision date. Repeating the evidence on the final weights shows a weakness we report rather than hide: at 87.5–92.5% the fit is rejected or borderline at 5%, while 95% fits well but gives a heavier tail (ξ of 0.41 against 0.27).

The practical effect is contained. 99% VaR moves only from 1.37% to 1.44% across thresholds, but 99% ES moves from 2.23% to 2.44%, and it would be about 7% higher at the better-fitting 95% threshold. The decision does not rest on this estimate: the ES limit uses the through-the-cycle figure, and the champion applies EVT to GARCH residuals.

6. Model validation

Design

Every model forecasts each day from data up to the day before, using the weights actually held. Forecasts are refitted every 5 days on a 500-day trailing window. We selected on 2019–2022 and tested once on 2023–2026, after a git tag (methodology-freeze-v1) had frozen the method.

Tests

Kupiec's proportion-of-failures test asks whether the number of breaches x in T days matches the coverage p.

LR_uc = −2 ln [ (1 − p)^(T − x) p^x / ( (1 − x/T)^(T − x) (x/T)^x ) ] ~ χ²(1)

Christoffersen's independence test asks whether breaches cluster, using the transition counts between breach and no-breach days. Added to Kupiec's statistic it gives conditional coverage, χ²(2). The Basel traffic light counts 99% breaches in each 250-day window: green up to 4, yellow 5–9, red 10 or more. We add the Acerbi-Székely Z2 test for ES, since VaR tests cannot see ES. Models that pass are ranked by the pinball loss, a proper score for quantiles.

L = (y − q)(τ − 1{y < q})

Selection on 2019–2022

A model passes if it clears Kupiec and conditional coverage at 5%, has no red rolling window, and has a green Z2. The rule, fixed in advance, picks the lowest pinball loss among those that pass.

Under stress the gap between models is wide. Models without a volatility forecast were overrun in 2008 and 2022, with up to nine times the expected breaches. One-shot test on 2023–2026

The champion passed: 9 breaches against 9.6 expected over 955 days, Kupiec p = 0.86, conditional coverage p = 0.19, and the lowest pinball loss of eleven models (Table 10). Only three models passed; those without GJR's asymmetry failed because their breaches clustered. A separately written notebook reproduced the breaches and losses.

What the test does not show

About ten expected breaches give limited power, so the test shows the model is not detectably wrong, not that it is right. Over all of 2008–2026 the joint coverage test falls just below 5% because of clustering in 2008.

7. Champion–challenger and final test

A risk model should be challenged. We tested machine-learning challengers for 99% VaR under a rule fixed in advance: a challenger must be better on validation, pass coverage, and stay within 2% of the champion's loss on the final test. Comparisons used Diebold-Mariano tests with a Holm correction and a model confidence set.

LightGBM and an LSTM failed coverage on validation in every configuration and were dropped. Linear quantile regression stayed as the reported challenger: on validation it had 19 breaches (Kupiec p = 0.01), so it failed coverage, and its difference from the champion was not significant (Holm-adjusted p = 1.0). On the final test it passed coverage, but its loss was 5.4% worse than the champion's, so it was not adopted. No machine-learning model improved calibrated 99% forecasts for this portfolio, which is a useful result rather than a failed experiment.

Hypothetical scenario: Hormuz stagflation 2027

Our scenario is the crisis bonds cannot hedge. Over three quarters the 10-year yield rises 250 bp, oil 40%, VIX 25 points, the dollar 8% and credit spreads 150 bp, and AGG then has one panic day of −5%, like 10 October 2008 when bond-fund prices fell below the value of their bonds.

Fund moves come from factor betas estimated on stressed weeks. Each fund's log move is converted to a simple return before aggregation, because only simple returns add across assets. The scenario costs 21.0%. It is the most severe candidate still within history, and the mapping reproduced 2022 at 17.8% predicted against 18.8% actual. A version beyond the historical record would cost about 25.3%, just over the budget, and we show it to the committee rather than leave it out.

The aggregation was corrected after the methodology freeze. This moved the scenario from 21.09% to 21.04% (USD 4,363), did not touch the test or the verdict, and is logged in the freeze record with both figures.

Innovation and optional risks

We studied four extensions on the 35% candidate, using data to 2022.

9. Risk budget and recommendation

Position sizing

Position sizing uses each holding's marginal contribution to ES. Euler's theorem splits the total, so the contributions add up exactly.

ESᵢ = wᵢ × E[ −Rᵢ | L ≥ VaR ] and Σ ESᵢ = ES

No fund carries more than 40% of the tail risk, so the sizing rule moved nothing. The largest share is SPY at 33%. XLE is the most risk-dense holding, at three times its weight. AGG is 65% of the money but 16% of the tail risk, which is how a conservative portfolio should look.

Verdict

The rule has three outcomes. ACCEPT if a portfolio meets the objective inside every limit. ADJUST THE RISK LIMIT if one would with the crisis budget loosened by at most 5 points. REJECT otherwise. We recorded in advance that the verdict would turn on real yields: on end-2022 yields the same rule gave REJECT, and a 10-year TIPS yield of about 2.58% would flip it. On the decision date it was 2.66%, and the rule gave ACCEPT at 35% equity.

How close it is

ACCEPT is lost if the real yield falls by 0.15 points or if the equity premium is below 4.15%, against our 4.5%. Across 18 assumption combinations it is ACCEPT in 7, ADJUST in 4 and REJECT in 7. The largest single swing is tax: if AGG's interest is taxed at 15%, the base case becomes REJECT.

Over ten years, a block bootstrap puts the chance of meeting the objective at about 49%, with a median real return of 3.5% a year and an 8% chance that real value ends below 80% of today's. These figures are context, not part of the rule.

Why this suits a conservative institution

  • It protects capital first. Each limit holds, and the worst crisis on record stays inside the budget.
  • Income pays the scholarships. Dividends and interest of about USD 327,000 a year (3.27%) cover the 3% spending rate, so the fund does not need to sell in a crisis.
  • It is simple and liquid. Four liquid ETFs and a quarterly rebalance.
  • Its weaknesses are known. The committee sees in advance where the bond hedge fails and what would change the answer.

Governance

  • Rebalance to target weights every quarter.
  • If the daily risk forecast stays above the limit for 5 days, the committee meets. Nothing is sold automatically, because an automatic de-risking rule made results worse out of sample.
  • Review the spending rate, the limits and the return assumptions every year.
  • Obtain tax advice on AGG's interest before investing.

10. Assumptions and limitations

The table lists each assumption, why it matters and what we do about it. Three matter most:

  • The return objective rests on an assumed equity premium and on AGG's tax treatment.
  • Models cannot price a crisis unlike any in the data, and the sample has no 1970s-style inflation.
  • The fund measures everything in US dollars while paying scholarships in rupees.
