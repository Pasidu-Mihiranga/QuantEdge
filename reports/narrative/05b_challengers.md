A risk model should be challenged. We tested machine-learning challengers for 99% VaR under a rule fixed in advance: a challenger must be better on validation, pass coverage, and stay within 2% of the champion's loss on the final test. Comparisons used Diebold-Mariano tests with a Holm correction and a model confidence set.

LightGBM and an LSTM failed coverage on validation in every configuration and were dropped. Linear quantile regression stayed as the reported challenger: on validation it had 19 breaches (Kupiec p = 0.01), so it failed coverage, and its difference from the champion was not significant (Holm-adjusted p = 1.0). On the final test it passed coverage, but its loss was 5.4% worse than the champion's, so it was not adopted. No machine-learning model improved calibrated 99% forecasts for this portfolio, which is a useful result rather than a failed experiment.
