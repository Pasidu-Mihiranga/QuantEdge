We compare historical, parametric and Monte Carlo VaR at 95% and 99%, and add GARCH-based and EVT models. They disagree most at 99%, for three reasons: fat tails, which a normal model understates; changing volatility, which historical windows absorb slowly; and the recent calm, which volatility-aware models reflect at once.

Expected Shortfall is reported alongside VaR because VaR says nothing about losses beyond it and is not subadditive, as our two-bond example shows. The committee's tail limit is therefore set on ES.

For EVT we fit a Generalised Pareto distribution above the 90% quantile. The threshold is justified by parameter stability, goodness-of-fit tests and sensitivity to alternative rules, and 99% figures barely depend on it.

For the recommended portfolio at the decision date, the 1-in-100-day VaR is about USD 75,700, ES USD 100,900 and the EVT estimate USD 180,900. These reflect a calm market; the through-the-cycle ES of USD 208,300 is the figure tested against the limit.
