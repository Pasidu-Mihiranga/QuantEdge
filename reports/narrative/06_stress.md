### Historical replays

We replay each crisis with today's target weights, rebalanced daily. This is the cautious convention, since it keeps the risky funds at weight while they fall.

- **GFC 2008:** 23.9%, the closest to the budget. Bonds helped.
- **COVID 2020:** 18.8%, deepened by the energy holding as oil collapsed.
- **Rate shock 2022:** 13.8%. Stocks and bonds fell together, so the bond hedge failed.
- **Recent episodes:** the 2025 tariff shock cost 5.7% and the 2026 Hormuz shock 2.9%, because energy gained.

### Hypothetical scenario: Hormuz stagflation 2027

Our scenario is the crisis bonds cannot hedge. Over three quarters the 10-year yield rises 250 bp, oil 40%, VIX 25 points, the dollar 8% and credit spreads 150 bp, and AGG then has one panic day of −5%, like 10 October 2008 when bond-fund prices fell below the value of their bonds.

Fund moves come from factor betas estimated on stressed weeks. Each fund's log move is converted to a simple return before aggregation, because only simple returns add across assets. The scenario costs **21.0%**. It is the most severe candidate still within history, and the mapping reproduced 2022 at 17.8% predicted against 18.8% actual. A version beyond the historical record would cost about 25.3%, just over the budget, and we show it to the committee rather than leave it out.

The aggregation was corrected after the methodology freeze. This moved the scenario from 21.09% to 21.04% (USD 4,363), did not touch the test or the verdict, and is logged in the freeze record with both figures.

### Innovation and optional risks

We studied four extensions on the 35% candidate, using data to 2022.

{{innovation_facts}}

{{innovation}}

The volatility-target overlay into AGG raised return and cut drawdown on this data, at three times the turnover. We also tested an automatic de-risking trigger: it helped on 2008–2018, where it was designed, but made results worse on 2019–2022 for every setting. We therefore adopted neither. The committee meets when risk stays high, and nothing is sold automatically.

### Crowd simulation

We also gave the same event to a MiroFish agent simulation, in which AI investors react and describe the market moves. Their answers set only direction and strength; the sizes come from history over the same three quarters, and the moves pass through the same stress test. Three 40-round runs gave losses of 15.6–17.8%, an average of 17.1%, against 21.0% for our scenario. This is a comparison only: it is not tested against real events and is not used in the recommendation.
