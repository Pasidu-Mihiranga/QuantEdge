D1 · D10

Return objective: CPI + 3.5% a year (3% spending + 0.5% real growth)

JudgeWhy do you spend 3% when most endowments spend 4–5%? And why should we accept your portfolio at all?

Our answerThe booklet asks the endowment to preserve and grow its capital, and leaves the return objective to us. A conservative portfolio of these seven ETFs can sustain about 3% spending plus 0.5% real growth only if real bond yields are about 1 pp above their end-2022 level; at end-2022 levels it sustains about 2%. A peer-level 4% would need far more equity risk than this committee accepts. So we set CPI + 3.5% and the verdict is ACCEPT if the recommended point meets it at the decision date, otherwise ADJUST THE RISK LIMIT, with its dollar cost.

Booklet
p. 3 “preserve and grow its capital … sustainable real growth”; p. 4 responsibility 2 “determine an appropriate return objective”; p. 8 memo options.
Robustness
At 3% spending the 10-year chance of losing a fifth of real value is about 26% (about 40% at 4%), even in the low-yield case. The verdict turns on the decision-date real yield (table below), computed once after the freeze.
D2

Crisis budget: the portfolio may lose at most 25% in a crisis

JudgeWhy 25%? Isn't that a lot for a conservative endowment?

Our answerIt is the largest loss in a GFC-type crisis that still lets the fund grow in real terms. A 20% budget allows only about 24% equity and cannot fund any real growth; above 25% the extra equity buys almost nothing after costs. Each 10 points of equity bought about 0.35 pp a year of return and cost about 3.3 pp more at the bottom of a crisis (about USD 35,000 a year for USD 330,000 more at the trough). A 35/65 mix is conservative by industry standards.

Booklet
p. 3 “avoiding large losses is more important” and “balance capital preservation, income generation, and sustainable real growth”; p. 4 responsibility 3.
Robustness
The volatility (8.4%) and 99% ES (2.23%) limits are calibrated from this one budget, so they no longer contradict each other. 20% is shown as a sensitivity.
D3

Caps: AGG at most 65%, each sector or EM fund at most 10%, gold at most 15%, equity at most 50%

JudgeWhy let one bond fund hold 65% of the endowment?

Our answerAGG holds thousands of bonds, so the cap guards against interest-rate concentration, not issuer risk. Moving it between 60% and 70% changes the mix but not the risk (volatility stays at 9.8% at 30% equity). From 35% equity no cap binds, so the recommendation is driven by risk, not by a number we typed.

Booklet
p. 4 “a single fund or any combination of eligible funds”; p. 4 “diversification benefits”.
Robustness
Cap sensitivity ±5 pp shown below; the IPS change log records every earlier cap change.
D4

Equity risk premium 4.5% and AGG spread 0.5%, stated as assumptions

JudgeWhere does your 4.5% equity premium come from? Isn't that a data source outside the approved list?

Our answerIt is a disclosed assumption, not data. It is anchored on approved Yahoo and FRED data and only cross-checked against published estimates (roughly 4.2–5.5% in 2025–26). More importantly, the verdict does not depend on it: at end-2022 levels even an ERP of 7.75% would be needed for 3% spending, and in the real-yield scenario the verdict is the same across 3.5–5.5%.

Booklet
p. 9 “using an undisclosed or proprietary data source is a rubric violation”.
Robustness
Break-even ERP far outside the plausible range; the same with a lower AGG spread and with AGG taxed.
D5

Hypothetical shock: a stagflation episode over about three quarters, peak to trough

JudgeWhy not a one-day shock?

Our answerReal crises unfold at different speeds. The GFC lost 81% of its damage within its worst quarter, but 2022 was a slow grind: only about half its loss came within any one quarter. A one-day shock would understate a 2022-type stagflation. Measuring the scenario peak to trough also makes it directly comparable with the 25% crisis budget.

Booklet
p. 8 “one hypothetical scenario the team designs itself”; p. 9 FRED series for stress-scenario construction.
Robustness
The worst quarter of the episode is shown separately as its speed.
D8

Risk budget: no ETF may carry more than 40% of the portfolio's tail risk

JudgeHow did you size the positions?

Our answerWith each position's contribution to Expected Shortfall. In the 35% portfolio, EFA is 19% of the money but 47% of the worst-day risk, so we cap it and move the difference to SPY. International equity also cost return over 2008–2022, so there are two independent reasons for the change.

Booklet
p. 8 “risk budgeting and position sizing, using each position's marginal VaR/ES contribution”.
Robustness
The contributions add up exactly to total ES (Euler check). The cap binds at 30–35% equity, where the recommendation sits.
D9

Monitoring: an alert that convenes the committee, no automatic trading

JudgeWill you cut risk automatically when markets get dangerous?

Our answerWe tested that rule and it failed. It looked good on 2008–2018, but on 2019–2022 every version deepened the worst loss and cut the return: COVID was over before it fired, and the fund then missed the rebound. So the risk forecast raises an alert (5 days above the limit) and the committee decides.

Booklet
p. 10 innovation examples “regime-conditioned risk, optional risk overlays”.
Robustness
Designed on one period, confirmed on another; reported as a negative result.
Risk

How bad can a bad day get, and how sure are we?

JudgeWhat can you lose, in dollars?

Our answerFor the 35% portfolio on USD 10M: about USD 125,000–135,000 on a 1-in-100 day (three methods agree within 5%), about USD 175,000 on average on those days, and USD 430,000–520,000 on a once-in-a-decade day. We give a range for the rarest case on purpose: that is where any model is least certain.

Booklet
p. 5 “how bad losses could become; how confident you are”; Layers 2–4.
Robustness
The normal model under-states rare losses (83 exceptions against 38 expected over 2008–2022 with today's weights); the champion model is volatility-adjusted.
H4

Does the risk model work out of sample?

JudgeHow do you know your VaR is right?

Our answerWe tested six models on the portfolio we recommend, using 2019–2022 data they never saw. The four volatility-adjusted models pass Kupiec, Christoffersen and the traffic light; historical VaR gets the count right but its breaches bunch together, and the normal model fails. The pre-registered rule picks GJR conditional EVT (14 breaches against 10 expected), statistically tied with the runner-up. Every model fails in a COVID-speed crash, which is why we also hold a crisis budget and stress tests.

Booklet
p. 7 Layer 5 “Kupiec, Christoffersen, Basel traffic light”; Layer 4 “how different modelling approaches perform under stressed market conditions”.
Robustness
Chosen on four years, where Kupiec has 79% power (24% over one year). The 2023–2026 test runs once after the freeze.
D2 · D5

What happens in a crisis?

JudgeWhat would a 2008 or a 2022 do to the endowment?

Our answerWith today's 35% portfolio: a GFC repeat costs about USD 2.4M at the worst point (24.4%) and about 7 months to recover; COVID about 15%; 2022 about USD 1.9M (18.8%), half of it from bonds. Our stagflation scenario, with a liquidity day, costs 18.8%, or 21.2% at 2022-size rates; only a combined severe case breaks the 25% budget. The most likely route to a breach is a GFC-type deflationary crash, about a 1% chance per stress episode.

Booklet
p. 8 “historical crisis return paths … plus one hypothetical scenario”; p. 4 “practical stress testing” and “resilience during market crises”.
Robustness
The factor mapping reproduces 2022 (17.8% predicted vs 18.8% actual). Less equity does not protect against a rate shock.
G27

Does diversification help?

JudgeYour seven-ETF mix did not beat a simple SPY/AGG mix. Why diversify?

Our answerBecause the benefit is in risk, not return. Diversification removes about a quarter of the worst-day risk (about USD 77,000 on a bad day) and avoided 5.4 points of loss in the GFC. It has a known weak spot: in the 2022 rate shock it saved only 0.6 points, because stocks and bonds fell together.

Booklet
p. 4 “diversification benefits”.
Robustness
The saving shrinks from 32% of tail risk on calm days to 23% on crisis days; we show both.
H1

When does the bond hedge fail?

JudgeIsn't AGG a safe haven?

Our answerIn crashes and normal times, yes. In rate-hiking cycles, no: there the stock–bond correlation is about +0.3 instead of −0.2 (Newey-West p < 0.001; the same sign out of sample 1990–2004, but not significant). Our original idea, that high inflation alone breaks the hedge, was tested and not supported.

Booklet
p. 4 optional macro and market risks; p. 10 “regime-conditioned risk”.
Robustness
Evidence graded moderate: strong in-sample, directional out of sample, definition fixed before use.
H3

Is the risk model right?

JudgeHow do you know your volatility model works?

Our answerWe test its leftovers. After dividing returns by the GARCH volatility forecast, what remains should be random. The plain GARCH(1,1) used by the current champion leaves volatility clustering (p ≈ 0.007); GJR-GARCH, where losses raise volatility more than gains, passes (p ≈ 0.16) and fits better. GJR is compared with the champion on validation data before the freeze.

Booklet
Layer 4 “evaluate how different modelling approaches perform”; Layer 5 backtesting.
Robustness
Model choice is made on 2019–2022 validation data only; 2023–2026 is tested once after the freeze.
Limits

What this analysis does not claim

  • Expected returns are forward-looking estimates built from yields and an assumed equity premium; the realised 2008–2022 returns are shown as history, not as a forecast.
  • The sample starts in November 2004 (GLD's launch), so the 2000–2002 technology crash is not in the data.
  • In crises AGG can trade well below the value of its bonds (−6.8% on 10 October 2008 when yields implied about −0.3%); a forced seller gets the ETF price.
  • US withholding tax applies to dividends (15% under the treaty); whether AGG's interest dividends are exempt needs a tax adviser.
  • Model choices were made on 2019–2022 validation data, so their p-values are optimistic; only the one-shot 2023–2026 test is clean.
